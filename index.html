RideOn: Designing Semiautonomous Cycling for Blind and Low-Vision Riders

Anonymous Research Project

This project investigates a semi-autonomous, human-powered cycling platform designed to support blind and low-vision riders through shared autonomy, multimodal feedback, and real-world evaluation.

Overview

Cycling provides transportation, recreation, exercise, and independent mobility, but many cycling tasks rely heavily on visual perception. This project explores how semi-autonomous assistance can support blind and low-vision riders while preserving meaningful rider participation and control.

Shared Autonomy

The rider provides propulsion and remains actively engaged, while the system assists with steering and safety-critical braking when needed.

Multimodal Feedback

Audio and vibrotactile feedback communicate system actions and support rider awareness of the surrounding environment.

Real-World Evaluation

The platform was iteratively developed and evaluated in authentic cycling environments across formative studies and later-stage evaluation.
